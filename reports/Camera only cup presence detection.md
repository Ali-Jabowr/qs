# Track the chain, not the cup

Industrial machine vision does not detect carriers with a camera alone in the way this line needs. Commercial "sensorless" triggers exist, and the closest proven pattern is Cognex's image-trigger loop (a cheap detector on a fixed region of interest (ROI), several consecutive frames to confirm, then a separate inspection) ([US9651499](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9651499)). But every published cup, capsule or blister line I found keeps per-slot results in order with an **index signal, an encoder or a light barrier**, not the camera. The documented way these systems fail is a camera that fires only when it sees a product: empty slots never trigger it, and the results **fall out of sync with the belt** ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/resolving-slc-504-vision-inspection-missing-part-tracking)). For v1, the sound camera-only design is to treat the chain itself as the encoder. Measure displacement in a strip over the chain, use the chain's own stops as the trigger, count pitches from features that are there whether or not a cup is, and decide what is in each slot as a separate step. Telling an **empty clear cup from no cup** is the harder optical problem, and I found **no published accuracy figure for it**. Glare on polished steel defeats the textbook fixes (a backlight and a dark matte background), so the problem has to be solved with lighting before any classifier. The most promising cheap cue is crossed polarisers, with the steel acting as a mirror so light passes through the cup twice. It is a strong but **untested hypothesis**, and a bench afternoon with polariser film and real cups can confirm or kill it. Throughout, **Sourced** means a cited document says it, and **Inference** means it is my design reasoning from those sources.

## Vendors sell image triggers, but the published lines still sync to a machine index

Commercial smart cameras offer three kinds of trigger that need no external sensor. They differ in a way that matters. A **timed "internal" trigger** captures every N ms and knows nothing about parts (Keyence IV "Internal") ([Keyence IV manual](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/658109/keyence-iv-series.html?page=233)). **Free-run "Continuous"** capture grabs frames as fast as it can ([Cognex Image Settings](https://docs.cognex.com/is_573/web/EN/ezb/Content/EasyBuilder/EasyBuilderLite/ImageSettings.htm)). A **true image-based trigger** runs cheap tools on every frame and fires an inspection when a part appears. Cognex's Checker line has marketed this since 2004 as a photoeye replacement ([Control Global](https://www.controlglobal.com/industrynews/2004/6); [Cognex 2014](https://www.cognex.com/company/press-releases/2014/cognex-introduces-easy-to-use-vision-sensor-for-color-applications)), and Keyence's IV4 sells it as "AI Trigger", with no stated limits ([Keyence IV4](https://www.keyence.com/landing/lpc/iv4_iast.jsp)). The Cognex configurable-image-trigger patent describes the mechanism best. Presence, edge, pattern or histogram tools are evaluated on a continuous stream (about 200 acquisitions/s in its example). It can **require several consecutive passing frames** before firing, and it can detect on a cheap low-resolution stream and then capture a separate inspection image. The same patent names what went wrong with earlier image triggers: **"inaccurate acquisition counts", gating problems and "loss of synchronization"** ([US9651499](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9651499)).

Vendor documentation consistently says hardware triggers are the precise option. Software triggers are "not time accurate" ([CVB GenICam guide](https://help.commonvisionblox.com/GenICam-User-Guide/html_english_genicam_triggerconfigs.htm)), with latency in milliseconds against microseconds for hardware ([The Imaging Source](https://www.theimagingsource.com/en-us/resource/glossary/hardware-triggering-and-strobe-synchronization/)), and Basler recommends a hardware trigger whenever a signal exists ([Basler](https://docs.baslerweb.com/triggered-image-acquisition)). *Inference:* that jitter matters when you time an exposure on a moving part. Here the chain is stopped more than half the time, so the real risk is the one the Cognex patent names: **counting and sync errors**, not exposure timing. One practitioner warns that in continuous mode a part detected once stays in view and gets counted again ([MrPLC](https://mrplc.com/forums/topic/28862-problem-with-trigger-camera-keynce-with-s7-1200/)). A stop-and-go chain makes that the default case.

The industry evidence on separating an **empty slot** from **no slot result** points the same way. In a documented indexing-conveyor failure, the shift register advanced only on the camera's "evaluation done" bit. Empty slots produced no done bit, and tracking drifted after a few misses. The recommended fixes, from most to least robust, were: shift on every physical index whether or not a part is present; track presence separately; compute the read pointer from encoder or index counts; and compare expected against actual trigger counts ([IMD KB](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/resolving-slc-504-vision-inspection-missing-part-tracking)). Real lines confirm the pattern. A coffee-capsule line at 500/min triggers from a light barrier ([MVTec](https://www.mvtec.com/company/success-stories/cameras-capture-coffee-capsules-seidenader)). Simple rotary cup fillers use an **"electric eye"** for no-cup/no-fill ([ZONESUN](https://www.zonesunautopack.com/products/zs-fs100-automatic-rotary-filling-capping-sealing-machine)). Blister machines map camera to punch with a **stroke count typed into the HMI**, and a wrong entry rejects good cards and passes bad ones ([Jornen](https://jornen.com/?p=2361)). I found **no published camera-only pitch-tracking system on a cup filler**.

*Inference, and a direct flag for ADR-0028 ("v1 triggers on seeing it"):* a trigger that fires on the cup still fires on an *empty* cup, so empty-cup detection survives. But a gap slot produces no event at all. This is harmless only while the output is a per-event operator alert. It becomes the documented desync failure the moment results must map to physical slot positions, for example "the 3rd cup after the camera", per-batch counts, or a later reject point.

## The chain can be its own encoder: displacement, dwell and pitch from a strip

Measuring conveyor speed from video is well studied, but only for **continuous belt speed**, not slot counting. A 2025 study fusing RAFT optical flow with feature matching reports relative errors around **5.25–5.5%**. It also notes that low-texture belts starve classic feature matching ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-30488-2)). Robot conveyor tracking in practice still latches a **physical encoder** at capture time (Epson, Mitsubishi) ([Epson](https://download.epson.biz/robots/ww/data/html/UsersGuide/en/170/170-010/170-010-030.html); [Mitsubishi](https://www.mitsubishielectric.com/fa/document/technews/robot/bfp-a6079_0407e/bfp-a6079_0407e.pdf)). A "virtual encoder" exists mostly in patents ([ipiq](https://ipiq.io/patents/1452145)). Two sourced building blocks transfer directly. First, Teledyne's line-scan sync patent reduces each line of an ROI to its mean to get a 1-D signal. It takes the **period from FFT autocorrelation peak spacing** and corrects the rate multiplicatively, with clamps ([US11262316B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11262316B2/en)). Second, phase correlation gives frame-to-frame displacement with a confidence value ([OpenMV](https://docs.openmv.io/openmvcam/tutorial/image/matching/displacement-and-keypoints.html)), and with peak fitting it reaches about **0.01 px** ([Argyriou](https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/42634/1/Argyriou-V-42634-AAM.pdf)). The only sourced area-camera "virtual light barrier" is a small 2022 study on a **chain conveyor with a 640×480 USB webcam**. It takes the median L (Lab) value in an ROI (about 5 when empty, 146–255 when an item crosses), smooths it with a moving average, counts rising edges, and reports **no miss or false-count rate** ([Castillo Alarcón et al. 2022](https://unaj.edu.pe/revista/index.php/vpin/article/download/223/126/668)). For counting, practitioners use detector + tracker + counting line ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/counting-objects-conveyor-belt/)). One demo claims correct single counts "even when the belt stops or reverses briefly", without figures ([oee.ai](https://www.oee.ai/en/video/cookie-counting-computer-vision)). Roboflow's own advice is to put the counting line **away from where items stop** ([Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/answers/running-total-for-video-or-shift)). On this line that is impossible, because the inspection point is where the chain dwells.

*Inference, the proposed v1 design.* The frame budget is generous. 56 cups/min is 1.07 s per pitch. If the chain moves less than half the time, it travels at least about 1.9 pitch/s while moving, which is about 16 frames per pitch of motion at 30 fps. That is enough for 1-D tracking, so **exposure, not frame rate, sets blur**. The design is a state machine on a strip ROI laid along the chain, preferably on chain links, pins or slot rims rather than cups:

1. Each frame, compute the 1-D profile shift (cross- or phase correlation) and integrate it into displacement.
2. Declare **STOPPED** after k frames with shift near zero.
3. On every moving→stopped transition, check that the displacement since the last stop is about one pitch or an integer multiple, advance the slot counter by that integer, and inspect the slot under the fixed ROI using the settled frames.
4. Keep a phase estimate (displacement mod pitch), corrected toward observed carrier edges in alpha-beta or PLL style. Reject corrections larger than about pitch/4 as outliers.
5. When foreground in the strip spikes (a hand), freeze counting, then re-acquire phase, and log any re-sync that needed a pitch jump.

This makes empty slots harmless, because the counter advances on chain motion, not on product. It also turns the stop that breaks line-crossing counters into the trigger. A space-time image (one line across the chain per frame, stacked into a "kymograph") makes stops, moves and pitch visible for debugging and calibration. The sourced IMD validation step carries over as a cheap guard: compare expected pitches against observed pitches per stop. Failure modes to test are creep starts read as stops, stop vibration of a few pixels, 50 Hz flicker beating against 30 fps (avoid it by locking exposure to a multiple of 10 ms or by DC-driving the station's own LED), and an empty slot combined with a missed edge, which gives a silent off-by-one. None of this is published practice. The literature search found **no paper or app note on phase-locking carriers on an indexing chain with a camera**, and **no hand-occlusion handling** for sensorless counting.

## Glare on polished steel breaks every textbook way of seeing clear plastic

Industry sees clear plastic in two ways. A **diffuse bright-field backlight** turns refracting walls and edges dark ([Microscan](https://files.microscan.com/_att/7e54b2b8-570c-4366-a740-5da5b0e5336c/lighting_tips_white_paper.pdf)), and the edge of a transparent object is its strongest signal in transmitted light ([Keyence IG manual](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/657976/keyence-ig-series.html?page=70)). **Dark-field or low-angle light** makes rims and edges scatter light into the lens, bright on a dark field. One case lit only the necks of clear bottles with a ring light under a vertical camera ([Vision Systems Design](https://www.vision-systems.com/boards-software/article/16738638/lighting-exposes-bottle-defects), snippet only). Empty-pocket detection on clear blisters is solved with a light plate **under the transparent web**: an empty pocket shows bright, a filled one dark ([Jornen](https://jornen.com/?p=2361); [Frain](https://www.fraingroup.com/wp-content/uploads/5-30-17-Vision-inspection-Whitepaper-Whitepaper.pdf)). Vendors warn that clear targets "burn through" or reflect unpredictably ([Banner](https://bannerengineering.com/ca/en/solutions/smart-sensors/consistent-detection-of-transparent-plastic-bottles-along-a-conv.html)). They also warn that a clear container can reflect enough light to **mimic an empty zone** ([US5828056](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5828056)), and that transparent edges can be "sometimes dark and sometimes very transparent" ([US5107131A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5107131)). **No vendor note covers clear plastic over polished metal.**

*Inference:* each textbook method assumes a background this line does not have. A steel channel cannot be backlit from below. Dark-field needs a matte dark floor, while polished steel reflects the low-angle ring wherever scratches, seams or links line up, which creates bright clutter shaped like a rim. Overhead coaxial or bright-field light is the worst choice, because flat steel throws it straight back and saturates the image. From an overhead camera the cup wall is seen almost edge-on, so the **rim** is the main geometric target. That explains why a learned model on raw frames would latch onto reflection patterns. It also explains why the current `localize` finds nothing in 2,883 line frames. The cup has no stable appearance of its own, only what the steel and the lights paint on it.

Two cheap optical routes turn the steel from a problem into a part of the instrument. The first is **cross-polarisation**. A polariser on the light and one on the lens at 90° remove specular glare, but every light source, including ambient light, must be polarised ([VA Imaging](https://va-imaging.com/en-us/blogs/machine-vision-knowledge-center/polarization-in-your-machine-vision-application)). The cost is about **10x less light on reflective items (1 ms → 10 ms exposure) and 6–10x on diffuse ones** (same source). Broadband sources also need UV/NIR-block filters ([EPIC Systems](https://epicsysinc.com/blog/eliminating-glare-with-polarization/)). Plastics are birefringent: between crossed polarisers, internal stress shows as **bright, coloured strain patterns** ([Mecan polariscope](https://www.mecanusa.com/Strain-Inspection-Polariscope/Polariscope-Strain-Inspection-MRV-ST.htm)). Container patents use stress-induced polarisation change as a bright signal ([US6067155](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6067155)), and NI uses a polarised backlight with a crossed lens polariser for transparent seals ([NI](https://www.ni.com/en-il/innovations/white-papers/06/inspecting-transparent-materials-for-material-defects.html)). *Inference:* polished steel reflects specularly and keeps polarisation, so with crossed polarisers it should go dark, while a moulded PP/PET cup in a double-pass path (down through the cup, off the steel mirror, back up through the cup) rotates the polarisation and lights up. That gives **positive contrast for an empty cup over a dark floor**, which is exactly the signal needed. The risks are thin low-stress cups (the polariscope vendor says visibility depends on thickness and stress), brushed or scratched steel and **starch dust**, both of which depolarise light and leak through as bright noise, and the light loss that pushes exposure toward motion blur.

The second route is **refraction against a known pattern**. Transparent objects distort the background behind them according to their shape and refractive index ([PMC5030754](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5030754)). Ordinary features fail on glass because it takes on the look of its background, while modelling the distortion works ([Maeno et al., CVPR 2013](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2013/html/Maeno_Light_Field_Distortion_2013_CVPR_paper.html)). Background-oriented schlieren measures that distortion against a reference image by cross-correlation or optical flow, with equivalent results ([DLR 2024](https://elib.dlr.de/206245/1/ELIB-Eintrag-2024-RaffelM-206245-PaperPublished.pdf)). *Inference:* a cup is a far stronger refractor than the gas flows schlieren targets. A fixed pattern seen through the slot should shift by many pixels at the wall, rim and floor radius, against a per-slot empty reference. The pattern could be printed or etched on a food-safe plate in the channel, or placed where the steel mirror reflects it into the camera (deflectometry). This route could separate three states: no distortion means no cup, a ring distortion with the floor pattern still visible means an empty cup, and a hidden pattern means a filled cup. Its risks are chain position jitter against the reference, starch or water films changing the texture, and blur smearing any pattern finer than the blur length. **No industrial report** uses this for conveyor presence.

UV fluorescence and SWIR are ruled out for a USB camera. PET fluoresces weakly at 325–420 nm ([Shimadzu](https://www.analytik-news.de/en/papers/pdf/shimadzu50e.pdf)), PP essentially not at all without dye ([PMC12292781](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12292781/)), and food residue such as milk fluoresces more strongly than plastic ([Woolley & Hill](https://core.ac.uk/download/288353969.pdf)). The plastic bands near **1200/1700 nm** and water at **1450 nm** need an InGaAs SWIR camera ([Sci Rep 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-39754-7.pdf); [JAI](https://www.jai.com/technology/understanding-swir-technology/)). A Sony Polarsens camera computes per-pixel degree and angle of polarisation in one shot ([LUCID](https://thinklucid.com/?p=9042)), but it would replace the C920 and sits outside the re-orderable hardware rule unless a Hikrobot model exists. That is unverified.

## Filled vs empty is the easy half, and learned models only help after the optics

From overhead, "filled" means the product hides or changes the view inside the cup outline. Opaque моти adds texture and colour that an empty cup does not. *Inference:* that makes filled vs empty the easier distinction, and empty vs absent the hard one. Side-view meniscus methods ([US11803983](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11803983); [Sensors 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8069151)) do not apply to an overhead view. Pepperl+Fuchs warns that **low-contrast white product** in white trays defeats plain 2D vision, and falls back to a laser-line height sensor ([Pepperl+Fuchs](https://pepperl-fuchs.com/usa/en/34805.htm), snippet only). Starch-dusted dough on bright steel is a plausible case of the same problem.

The learned-model literature gives three useful results. First, open-world transparent segmentation is still hard. Trans4Trans reaches **75.14% mIoU** on Trans10K-v2 at about 34 GFLOPs ([arXiv 2108.09174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.09174)), the dataset authors call the task "largely unsolved" ([arXiv 2003.13948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13948v3)), and SAM segments what lies *behind* glass ([arXiv 2305.00278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00278v1)). These models solve a harder problem than a fixed slot ROI and do not fit an N100 budget. Second, **anomaly detection cannot do the 3-way split**. With "full cup" as normal, both empty and absent are anomalies. Missing objects are "logical" anomalies, where EfficientAD scores about **86 AUROC** and PatchCore **75.8** on MVTec LOCO ([EfficientAD, arXiv 2303.14535](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.14535v3)). MVTec AD 2, which adds transparent objects and lighting shifts, keeps state-of-the-art methods **below 60% AU-PRO** ([arXiv 2503.21622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21622v1)). PatchCore therefore stays the "something odd in this slot" second opinion, not the empty-cup detector. Third, the per-slot crop classifier is an established pattern: a patent classifies each tray-slot crop as empty or occupied with a random forest ([US10140705](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10140705)). A 2025 catering-line YOLO study fixed confusion between clear plastic and the background by adding **transparent-only training images** ([arXiv 2505.11879](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11879)). Under blur, aggregating predictions over frames beat single-frame CNNs by **17.6–22.4% F1**, but beat plain voting by only **about 2%** ([Sensors 2024](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/14/4438)). Most of the gain comes from using several frames at all. The stopped chain hands this line many sharp frames per slot for free.

Independent public numbers for empty-pocket or empty-container detection are **essentially absent**. Vendors claim around 99.5–99.6% ([iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/ai-vision-camera/ai-vision-pharma-bliste-pack-inspection)), and the practitioner advice is to count misses and false positives separately in a large controlled run ([IMD](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/sensing-clear-polyethylene-film-sensor-selection-configuration-for-bagging-applications)). *Inference:* with empties at 1–3% of traffic, a model can look 97–99% accurate by never saying "empty". The client's 95% therefore has to be measured as **recall on staged empties and gaps** on a held-out session, never as overall frame accuracy.

## Ranked options for empty cup vs no cup, and the bench tests that decide

Ranking criterion (*inference*): the chance of a clean, explainable separation under glare and dust, per unit of cost and schedule risk, on a C920 with its own light and diffuser. The slot-tracking design in section 2 is a prerequisite for all of them, not an alternative. Each option is judged per inspected slot.

| Rank | Option | What is sourced vs inferred | Evidence strength | Cost / risk on a USB setup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Crossed polarisers, double-pass off the steel** (film on the light, film on the C920 lens, rotated to extinction) | Sourced: glare suppression, plastic birefringence, polarised container inspection. Inferred: steel as mirror, positive cup contrast, starch behaviour | Medium on physics, **zero on this application** | Film costs almost nothing. **6–10x light loss** fights blur, so a much brighter LED is needed. Film must sit outside the food zone or behind a food-safe cover. Starch and brushed steel leak light |
| 2 | **Per-slot crop classifier + multi-frame vote** on existing diffuse lighting (none / empty / filled, or features + logistic regression) | Sourced: per-slot crop classifier pattern, transparent-only training fix, voting gain. Inferred: that the current optics carry enough signal | Medium for the pattern, **unknown for clear cup on steel** | Cheapest: data already exists (860 photos, 29 min video). Risk: it learns glare positions and breaks under a lighting shift (MVTec AD 2) |
| 3 | **Pattern / refraction reference** (patterned plate or mirror-placed panel, NCC or Farneback flow against the empty-slot reference) | Sourced: refraction distorts backgrounds, schlieren cross-correlation. Inferred: any use for conveyor presence | Medium in the lab, **no industrial precedent** | Low parts cost. Needs a food-safe pattern surface in or over the channel (ХАССП sign-off). Sensitive to chain jitter, starch and water films, blur |
| 4 | **Low-angle dark-field rim detection** (ring or bar at grazing angle, find the circular rim at the expected slot position) | Sourced: dark-field shows clear bottle rims. Inferred: failure on polished steel | Medium on matte backgrounds, **weak here** | Cheap. High risk of steel clutter that looks like a rim. Worth testing only together with option 1 |
| 5 | **Anomaly detection (PatchCore / EfficientAD)** as the decider | Sourced: weak on logical/missing anomalies and under lighting shift | Strong evidence **against** | Already in the stack. Keep only as a second opinion |
| — | Deferred alternative: **tap the existing photo sensor** for index and presence | Sourced: index/encoder/light barrier is what industry uses | Strong | Outside v1 by team decision. It is the fallback if the bench tests below fail |
| — | Ruled out for v1: UV fluorescence, SWIR, Polarsens camera | Sourced: weak PP/PET signal; SWIR needs InGaAs | — | New camera class, food-zone UV issues |

The cheapest tests that would decide between these, in order (all *inference*, designed from the sources above):

**Test 0, zero cost, on data already on disk.** Crop the slot ROI from the 860 line photos. Label none / empty / filled. Check whether simple per-crop statistics (mean, standard deviation, edge density, ring-band gradient) or a frozen-backbone embedding plus logistic regression separate *none* from *empty* on a held-out session. If they do, option 2 is viable on today's optics. If they don't, the optics have to change. In the same pass, build a kymograph from the 29 min video to measure pitch in pixels, the stop/move duty cycle and the chain speed while moving. The speed gives the real blur budget: blur px = speed × exposure ÷ mm per px.

**Test 1, polariser film, one afternoon.** Use a polished stainless sheet (or the channel on the next visit), real empty and filled cups, an LED panel with linear film, and film on the C920 lens rotated to extinction, with C920 exposure locked manually. Measure the grey-level contrast of empty cup against bare steel. Repeat with steel dusted with starch, with a wiped wet film, and at the exposure the blur budget from Test 0 allows. A clear decision rule: the cup is bright above dusted bare steel by a margin larger than the dust's own variance. If it is, option 1 wins and option 2 becomes a classifier on much cleaner input.

**Test 2, printed pattern, same afternoon.** Place a printed checker or random-dot sheet under a cup on the bench and compute NCC or optical flow against an empty reference. Repeat with the pattern placed where the steel reflects it. Score the displacement magnitude for empty cup vs none, and re-test after shifting the reference by ±2 px to mimic chain jitter.

**Test 3, after a winner exists.** Run a staged acceptance on the line: known empty and gap positions, counted separately for misses and false alarms, with the expected-vs-observed pitch log running.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the risk sits. The trigger problem has a known industrial answer, which is to count machine positions and not products, and the only new step is having the camera act as the position source. The chain's stop-go motion, which looks like a nuisance, is what makes that workable, because each dwell is a free, blur-free trigger. Empty vs absent is the open problem. No public number exists for it, and the cue that best fits this hardware, birefringence seen through a steel mirror, has never been reported on food cups. That makes the polariser bench test the highest-value experiment available: it costs almost nothing and decides whether the line needs new optics or only a classifier.

One finding bears directly on the current design. A trigger that fires on the cup copies the exact failure the industry sources warn about. That is acceptable while v1 only raises per-event operator alerts, but it should be replaced by chain-displacement counting before any result is tied to a slot position, a batch count or a reject point. If Tests 0–2 all fail to separate empty from absent with margin, that is the evidence-based moment to reopen the deferred photo-sensor option, rather than adding model capacity to a signal the optics never captured.
